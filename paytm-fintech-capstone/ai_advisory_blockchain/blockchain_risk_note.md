# Blockchain / Crypto Risk Analysis Appendix

## 1. Stablecoin and DeFi/DAO Governance Risk

A hypothetical **Paytm Crypto Insights** watchlist should treat stablecoins and DeFi/DAO products as higher-risk information areas rather than presenting them as automatically safe or stable. A stablecoin is designed to maintain a value relative to a reference asset, but its stability depends on the quality and liquidity of its reserves, redemption arrangements, issuer, and supporting infrastructure. A loss of confidence can create redemption pressure and cause the token to trade away from its intended value. Stablecoins can also face operational, cyber, interoperability, and financial-integrity risks.

DeFi introduces a different set of risks because financial functions may be implemented through smart contracts and permissionless networks rather than traditional intermediaries. Smart-contract failures, oracle problems, leverage, liquidity mismatches, and rapid liquidations can amplify losses. DeFi also does not necessarily eliminate governance concentration. In a DAO, governance tokens may be concentrated among a relatively small group of holders, meaning that the practical control of a supposedly decentralised protocol can remain concentrated.

For a retail-facing advisory product, Paytm Money should therefore display clear risk labels and avoid describing stablecoins, DeFi, or DAOs as equivalent to bank deposits or regulated investment products. Any watchlist feature should also distinguish between stablecoin type, reserve quality, redemption mechanism, protocol security, leverage, and governance concentration.

## 2. Crypto as an Asset-Class Recommendation

For the Paytm Money advisory product, my base recommendation is a **0% strategic allocation to cryptocurrency for the standard retail portfolio**. This recommendation is deliberately conservative. CAPM-style portfolio construction does not automatically make an asset attractive simply because it has diversification potential. Cryptoassets generally lack conventional intrinsic cash flows such as dividends, can experience heavy-tailed and highly asymmetric returns, and may involve substantial transaction, custody, regulatory, and operational risks.

The portfolio agent in this project already bases allocations on prescribed CAPM calculations using beta, expected market return, risk-free rate, and portfolio variance. A crypto allocation would therefore need a strong risk-adjusted justification rather than being added simply because its correlation with traditional assets may be low or negative. Survivorship bias and the possibility of large drawdowns also make historical crypto returns difficult to treat as a reliable forward-looking assumption.

A 0% strategic allocation does not mean that cryptocurrency has no technological or economic value. It means that, for a retail wealth-advisory product, the default recommendation should prioritize capital preservation and transparent risk-adjusted portfolio construction. If a future product permits a speculative allocation, it should be separately labelled, capped at a small percentage, and accompanied by explicit loss-risk and volatility disclosures rather than being included in the core recommended portfolio.

## 3. T.A.N.G. Social-Engineering Risk Analysis

For a UPI/wallet + lending + wealth platform, two particularly relevant social-engineering vectors are **Authority** and **Greed**.

### A. Authority

Fraudsters can impersonate a bank employee, regulator, platform representative, police officer, or other trusted authority. They may create urgency and instruct a customer to approve a transfer, reveal an OTP, change a beneficiary, or move money to a supposedly protected account.

**Bank-side real-time defence:** use a risk-based transaction intervention that detects unusual high-value payments, new beneficiaries, device changes, and rapid changes in customer behaviour. When the combination is suspicious, pause or step-up the transaction and require verification through a trusted channel already registered with the bank rather than through the contact information supplied by the caller. This reduces the effectiveness of authority-based impersonation.

### B. Greed

Investment scams can exploit the customer's desire for unusually high or guaranteed returns. A fraudster may present a fake crypto investment, lending opportunity, or wealth product and pressure the customer to transfer funds before the opportunity disappears.

**Bank-side real-time defence:** detect unusual investment-related payment patterns, especially first-time beneficiaries, sudden high-value transfers, rapid account funding, or transactions inconsistent with the customer's normal behaviour. The bank can trigger a targeted scam warning and cooling-off or confirmation step before releasing the payment. The intervention should explain that guaranteed returns and urgent investment requests are warning signs and should encourage independent verification.

## Conclusion

The main principle for Paytm Money is that crypto and social-engineering risks should be treated as **risk-management problems, not only technology problems**. Stablecoins and DeFi can introduce market, liquidity, governance, operational, and financial-integrity risks, while social-engineering attacks exploit human behaviour such as trust, authority, urgency, and greed. A responsible advisory product should therefore combine transparent disclosures, conservative portfolio rules, behavioural monitoring, and human intervention for high-risk situations.
